The editorial workflow plugin for WordPress teams who outgrew Draft and Published

WordPress ships with two states for a story: draft and published. A working newsroom lives in the space between them. Editloft puts that space back, with custom statuses, an editorial calendar, and the comments and metadata your team already talks in.

What it is

An editorial layer for WordPress

Editloft adds an editorial layer to WordPress: the states a story moves through, a calendar to see it all in one place, editorial comments that stay separate from reader comments, and metadata your desk actually uses. It is a maintained fork of Edit Flow, the plugin that gave WordPress these ideas in the first place. I kept the editorial thinking and rebuilt the machinery underneath it for the WordPress that exists today.

Why a successor

Why Edit Flow needed a successor

Edit Flow was built by Automattic, Mohammad Jangda, and Daniel Bachhuber, and it was good. It gave WordPress custom post statuses, an editorial calendar, a story budget, and editorial comments, and plenty of newsrooms ran on it for years. Its copyright line runs 2009 to 2019, and that span tells the story. It was written for a WordPress that came before the block editor. The platform moved, as platforms do, and the code stayed where it was. None of that is a knock on the people who wrote it. Their work was solid enough that the sensible move was to build on it rather than start over.

Custom post statuses

Five statuses, not two

Out of the box, a post in WordPress is either a draft or published. That is fine for a personal blog and useless for a desk that runs ten stories at once. Where is each one? Who has it? Is it waiting on an editor or waiting on you?

Custom statuses answer that. Editloft restores the states a real editorial process moves through: Pitch, then Draft, then Needs review, then Ready, then Published. Think of a busy kitchen. Every order rides a ticket along the rail, from ordered to fired to plated to served, and anyone can glance at the rail and know exactly where every plate stands. Custom statuses do that for your stories. A glance tells you what is pitched, what is being written, what is sitting with an editor, and what is ready to go. Nobody has to ask.

What Editloft adds

The calendar, comments, and metadata your desk already uses

Calendar

Editorial calendar

Your stories laid out by date, so you can see the week or the month at a glance and move things when plans change.

Comments

Editorial comments

The conversation your team has about a story: notes, questions, and direction, kept on the post and kept apart from the comments your readers leave.

Metadata

Editorial metadata

The desk-level detail a story carries, the fields your team relies on to keep track of who and what and when.

Audit trail

Audit trail

Every status change is recorded with who made it and when, so a masthead can answer what happened to a story without guessing.

Modern WordPress

Built for the WordPress that exists today

Editloft is written for the current platform: the block editor, Full Site Editing (FSE, WordPress’s system for editing your whole site in blocks), and a supported PHP version. It requires WordPress 6.4 or newer and PHP 7.4 or newer. The point of the rebuild was to sit comfortably inside the editor your team already uses every day, rather than fight it.

Free and open source

Built on Edit Flow, with gratitude

Editloft is free to use and free to read. It is licensed under the GNU General Public License version 2 or later (GPLv2), the same license Edit Flow chose. That license is the reason this plugin can exist: the GPL grants anyone the right to fork the code and carry it forward, and it asks that the original credit travel with it. So it does. Editloft’s plugin header still carries the Jangda, Bachhuber, and Automattic copyright line, with portions copyright 2024 to 2025 Christopher Ross. A fork is a continuation, not a hostile act.

A note on Edit Flow compatibility

If you already run Edit Flow

Editloft and Edit Flow manage a lot of the same things, so run one or the other, not both at once. There is no automated import from Edit Flow yet, and a migration guide is planned. The support page has the honest, current details before you make the move.
