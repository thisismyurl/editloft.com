Status transitions

Writers submit. Editors decide what goes out.

WordPress roles already say who can edit. Status transitions apply that to the moves that matter: review, schedule, publish and unpublish.

Download freeAll features

Rules

A clear default that matches how desks work

Out of the box, anyone who can edit a post can send a draft to pending review, pull it back to draft, or trash unpublished work. Publishing, scheduling, making private and unpublishing are for editors and administrators.

  • Writers keep their flow: draft, then pending review
  • Editors publish, schedule and unpublish
  • Rules apply to posts and pages by default, and you can extend them to other post types
Draft→to Pending review, allowed for Pending review Anyone who can edit the post
Draft→to Published, allowed for Published Editors and administrators
Pending review→to Scheduled, allowed for Scheduled Editors and administrators
Scheduled→to Draft, allowed for Draft Editors and administrators
Published→to Draft, allowed for Draft Editors and administrators only

Clarity

People see what they can do

The block editor stops offering Publish to a role the rules will refuse, so nobody clicks a button that is going to fail. If a blocked move gets through another way, a REST save returns a 403 and the classic editor shows a notice on the next screen.

  • No silent reverts, no mystery errors
  • Clear wording: your role cannot move this post from one status to another, ask an editor

Flexibility

Yours to change, and safe by default

Different team, different rules. One filter, editloft_status_transition_graph, lets you reshape the whole graph. A status that is not in the graph is not gated, so adding a custom status never locks anyone out.

  • WP-CLI and scheduled publishes are never blocked
  • Every move, allowed or not, is easy to audit alongside the audit trail

Questions

Common questions

Who can publish by default?

Editors and administrators. Writers can move a draft to pending review and back, and can trash their own unpublished work.

What happens when someone is blocked?

In the block editor the Publish button is not offered to roles the rules would refuse, and a blocked REST save returns a clear 403. In the classic editor a notice explains that their role cannot make that move and to ask an editor.

Are WP-CLI and scheduled publishes blocked?

Never. There is no editor to route them through, so they are always allowed, and they are still written to the audit trail.

How do I change the rules?

Filter editloft_status_transition_graph. Add statuses, change which roles may make a move, or return an empty array to turn enforcement off.
