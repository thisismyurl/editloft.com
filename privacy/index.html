Privacy policy

The short version: Editloft is built to run on your own server, and it keeps your editorial data there. This page explains exactly what this website and the plugin do and do not collect, including the one feature that can send data off your site, which is off until you turn it on.

What this website collects

editloft.com is a set of static pages. It sets no cookies, runs no advertising, and includes no third-party tracking scripts. The site is hosted on Cloudflare Pages, and Cloudflare processes standard request information (such as your IP address and browser type) to deliver the pages and protect the site, as described in Cloudflare’s own privacy policy. Nothing you do on this website is tied back to you by us.

What the Editloft plugin collects

Editloft runs inside your WordPress site. The editorial information it manages, your custom statuses, editorial comments, editorial metadata, and the audit trail, is stored in your own WordPress database and stays there. Editloft includes no analytics and no usage tracking. By default, it does not send your content or your editorial data to me or to any third party.

The Cloud Sync feature (off by default)

Editloft ships with an optional Cloud Sync module for teams who want to connect their site to a hosted Editloft service for notifications, approval workflows, and related integrations. It is worth being precise about it:

  • It is off by default. It does nothing until you turn it on in the plugin settings and enter your own credentials.
  • When you enable it, it connects to the Editloft cloud service at cloud.editloft.com and syncs editorial activity, which can include your custom statuses, editorial comments, and related events.
  • If you never enable it, none of that data leaves your site through this feature.

You stay in control of whether any of your data goes off your own server. That is the point of keeping it opt-in.

Notifications you set up

Editloft can send notifications about editorial activity to destinations you configure, such as an email address or a Slack channel. Those messages go only where you tell them to go. If you do not set up a notification destination, nothing is sent.

Update checks

So it can tell you when a new version is available, Editloft checks GitHub, where its releases are published, to compare your installed version against the latest one. That check contacts GitHub and carries no personal data about you or your content.

Third parties

The services that may be involved, and only in the situations named above, are GitHub (where the code and releases live, and where update checks and downloads go), Cloudflare (which hosts this website), and, only if you turn them on, the Editloft cloud service and any notification destination such as Slack. Each has its own privacy policy that governs the data it handles.

Contact

Questions about privacy, or about what a specific feature does with data, are welcome. You can raise them through the support page, on the GitHub issue tracker, or through the Editloft page on thisismyurl.com.

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted here.
